Our Approach

The OmniFit Method

Every Santaluz client follows the same four-phase methodology: Assess, Correct, Build, Sustain. Your program starts with a $110 Performance Diagnostic and a comprehensive movement screening, not a workout. We identify exactly where your body needs attention before programming a single exercise.

Santaluz residents share a familiar profile: accomplished professionals in their 40s–60s who built impressive home gyms but haven't used them consistently, or have used them without expert programming. Many play golf at The Santaluz Club, hike the surrounding trails, or stay casually active, but carry years of desk-related dysfunction underneath.

Our corrective-first approach turns your existing home gym into a structured training program. We identify the postural imbalances, joint restrictions, and strength deficits that hold you back, then systematically resolve them while building functional strength you'll feel in your golf game, your energy levels, and your daily movement quality.

Questions About Training in Santaluz

How does in-home personal training work in Santaluz?

We come to your Santaluz home and train using your existing gym equipment, or bring professional-grade portable equipment if needed. Sessions are 45–60 minutes, fully programmed from your assessment data. We coordinate gate access with you in advance, and a per-session travel fee may apply depending on location. Full details on our in-home training page.

What does a personal trainer in Santaluz cost?

OmniFit Performance publishes its rates in full. One-on-one coaching at Teqneeq runs $250 to $1,395 per month on a 3-month program depending on tier (Essential, Momentum, Performance, or Peak), or $290 to $1,625 month-to-month. In-home coaching across San Diego County starts at $335 per month on a 3-month program. Couples training starts at $325 per month. Session packs run $135 to $175 per session depending on pack size and location; single sessions are $165 at Teqneeq or $195 in-home. Full tables on the training rates page.

Do I need a fully equipped home gym?

No. We bring professional-grade portable equipment when needed, so all you need is about 8×8 feet of clear space. That said, most Santaluz clients have substantial setups: power racks, cable machines, cardio equipment, and we design your program around whatever you have.

What is the Performance Diagnostic?

The OmniFit Performance Diagnostic ($110) uses the BodyStat 1500 MDD, a medical-grade bioimpedance device, to establish your body composition baseline: fat mass, lean mass, and hydration markers. Combined with a 9-point movement screen, it gives us objective data to build your program from and to measure real progress against at rescans. If you continue into a training package, the diagnostic fee is credited in full toward a 3-month package.

What certifications does your trainer hold?

Nemezio LopezPerez holds NASM-CPT, NASM-CES (Corrective Exercise Specialist), NASM-PES (Performance Enhancement Specialist), NASM-BCS (Behavior Change Specialist), NASM-CNC (Certified Nutrition Coach), and the ACE Corrective Exercise Specialist credential, plus Precision Nutrition certifications. All programming is evidence-based and assessment-driven.

Will this help my golf game?

Directly. Golf demands thoracic rotation, hip mobility, and core stability, all of which deteriorate with desk work and age. Our corrective-first approach restores the mobility your swing needs while building the rotational power and anti-extension strength that protects your lower back. Many clients report measurable improvement in their game within 2–3 months of consistent training.

How is OmniFit different from the trainers at the club?

Club trainers typically serve many members with general programming in a shared space. OmniFit provides fully individualized, assessment-driven training in the privacy of your own home. Your program is built from a $110 Performance Diagnostic and a 9-point movement screen specific to your body, tracked with monthly data reports, and adjusted based on measurable progress, not general fitness trends.
