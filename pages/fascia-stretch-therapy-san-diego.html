The Short Definition

The Tissue Behind the Name

What Fascia Is, and Why It Shapes How You Move

Fascia (say "FASH-uh") is the connective tissue that wraps and links everything in your body. Picture the membranes of an orange: one skin around the whole fruit, another around each segment, another around every tiny cell of juice. Your muscles are packaged the same way.

What changes fascia. Fascia responds to how it is used. Johns Hopkins Medicine describes it as designed to stretch as you move, and names too little movement day after day, and repetitive movement that overworks one part of the body, among the things that make it thicken and become sticky. It also changes with age: see the section for senior adults.

Sources for this section: Cleveland Clinic, Fascia, and Johns Hopkins Medicine on fascia. General anatomy, written for a general reader.

How It Works

How Fascia Stretch Therapy™ Works With Fascia

Most stretching picks one muscle and holds it. FST™ starts from the fascia instead. Because the tissue is one connected web, the stretching follows it across joints and through whole regions of the body, with a practitioner doing the moving for you.

  • Connected regions, not single musclesA stretch takes in a whole line of tissue, such as the hip, the leg and the lower back together, instead of isolating one muscle.
  • Movement instead of long holdsYou are moved smoothly into and out of each stretch, and through several angles, so the tissue is worked in the directions it runs.
  • Room in the jointGentle traction is part of the stretch, so the capsule of fascia around the joint is included, not only the muscle that crosses it.
  • Paced by your breathingThe stretching follows your breath and stays inside a range that feels comfortable. You stay relaxed and nothing is forced.
  • Adjusted as it goesNemezio reads how your body responds and adapts each stretch to you, visit by visit.
This is how the method approaches the body. It is not a promise of a result. At your First Visit your range of motion is measured before the session and again after it, so what you go on is your own two sets of measurements.

Senior Adults

Assisted Stretching for Senior Adults

Fascia Stretch Therapy™ is a great fit for senior adults. Flexibility is one of the things that changes most with age, and this is a way to work on it that asks very little of you: you lie on a table at home and the practitioner does the stretching.

Why it suits senior adults

  • You lie on a tableThere is no getting down to the floor and back up, and no position to hold on your own.
  • The practitioner does the workYou stay relaxed while Nemezio moves you. There is no routine to learn or remember.
  • It comes to youNo drive, no parking and no waiting room. The table is set up in your own home.
  • It goes at your paceIt is designed to be comfortable, and every stretch is adjusted to how you move that day.
  • You see your own measurementsYour range of motion is measured at the start and at the end of the First Visit.

It suits people who want to keep reaching the top shelf, turning to look over a shoulder in the car, getting down to the garden, or walking eighteen holes.

If you have a joint replacement or a medical condition, check with your physician before you book. The note at the foot of this page applies to every client.

The research behind the charts. Stathokostas and colleagues measured shoulder and hip range of motion in 436 adults aged 55 to 86 (205 men and 231 women). Stathokostas L, McDonald MW, Little RMD, Paterson DH. Flexibility of older adults aged 55–86 years and the influence of physical activity. J Aging Res. 2013;2013:743843. Wilke and colleagues used ultrasound to measure the thickness of the deep fascia in 35 healthy women, 18 aged about 22 and 17 aged about 69, and found that fascia thickness and flexibility were related. Wilke J, Macchi V, De Caro R, Stecco C. Fascia thickness, aging and flexibility: is there an association? J Anat. 2019;234(1):43–49. The National Institute on Aging names flexibility as one of four types of exercise that matter as people age, with endurance, strength and balance (National Institute on Aging). These sources describe how flexibility and fascia change with age in general. They are not studies of Fascia Stretch Therapy™, and OmniFit Performance cites them to explain the tissue, not to make any claim about outcomes.
