The Diagnostic

Why We Assess Before We Program

A workout written before anyone has watched you move is a guess. It may be a good guess, but it cannot account for the restriction in your left hip, the shoulder that only hurts under load, or the fact that your squat is being driven by the wrong muscles. Assessment is what turns a plan into your plan, and it is what makes progress measurable later rather than merely felt.

The Nine-Point Movement Screen

  • Overhead Squat Assessment — compensations in ankles, knees, hips, and shoulders under a loaded pattern
  • Single-Leg Squat — unilateral stability, hip strength, knee tracking
  • Push & Pull Patterns — shoulder mechanics and scapular stability under load
  • Posture Photos — front, side, and back baseline, re-scored quarterly
  • Joint Range of Motion — shoulders, hips, ankles, thoracic spine
  • Grip Strength — a reliable marker of functional strength, tracked monthly
  • BodyStat Body Composition — medical-grade bioimpedance via the BodyStat 1500 MDD: lean mass, fat mass, hydration
  • Core Stability — plank and anti-rotation testing
  • Gait Analysis — walking mechanics that reveal asymmetries and compensations

Resting Heart Rate and Blood Pressure

Two readings are taken at the diagnostic and repeated at each monthly review: resting heart rate and resting blood pressure. Both are simple, both are free to collect, and both give context that a scale cannot.

  • Resting heart rate — measured seated after five minutes of quiet, tracked as a trend rather than a single number
  • Resting blood pressure — recorded the same way each time, same arm, same posture
  • Trend, not diagnosis — these are training-context markers, not a medical assessment

Readings outside the ordinary range are a referral, not a programming adjustment. If a reading is high or unusual, the next step is your physician, and training continues only within whatever guidance they give.

Education, not diagnosis: the screen describes how you move and gives us a baseline to program from. OmniFit Performance does not diagnose conditions. If something in the screen suggests a problem beyond a trainer's scope, we say so and refer.
Protocol

The Desk-Posture Protocol

Most in-home clients sit for a living, and the pattern that produces is consistent enough to program against directly. Prolonged sitting holds some muscle groups short while their opposing partners sit long and under-used, and tissue adapts to the position it spends the most time in. The protocol runs in every session rather than as a warm-up bolted on the front.

Release and Lengthen

Hip flexors, chest and front-of-shoulder, and the upper trapezius. Soft-tissue work followed by targeted lengthening, kept inside the 3/10 pain ceiling.

Activate

Glutes, deep core, lower trapezius, and the mid-back. These are the partners that go quiet during long sitting hours, and they will not switch on by themselves under load.

Integrate and Load

Hinge, squat, push, and pull retrained so the right muscles fire in the right order, then progressively loaded once the pattern holds. Load is added to a pattern that works, never to one that does not.

Scope

What We Can and Cannot Coach Around

These are the situations OmniFit Performance genuinely works with in-home, described honestly. None of this is treatment, and none of it replaces the provider managing your care.

Coached Regularly

  • Desk-related posture patterns — forward head, rounded shoulders, tight hip flexors
  • Post-physical-therapy return to training — after discharge and clearance
  • Old injuries that healed but never fully rebuilt — previous sprains, strains, and surgeries
  • Joint discomfort under load — programming modified around a 3/10 pain ceiling
  • Deconditioning after a long break — returning gradually with progression tied to tolerance

Only Alongside Your Provider

  • Ongoing physical therapy — we coordinate rather than duplicate, with your permission
  • Managed chronic conditions — training within whatever limits your physician sets
  • Recent surgery — only after written clearance, and at the pace your surgeon specifies

Not In Scope

  • Undiagnosed pain — see a physician or Doctor of Physical Therapy first
  • Acute injury — this is treatment, not training
  • Diagnosis of any kind — OmniFit does not diagnose, and does not prescribe rehabilitation
  • Prenatal and postpartum programming — not a certification we hold
The honest version: a trainer who claims every condition is workable is telling you something about their marketing, not their scope. If a situation belongs with a clinician, the useful thing we can do is say so early and coordinate once you are cleared.
