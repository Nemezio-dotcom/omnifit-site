The Diagnostic

Why We Assess Before We Program

A workout written before anyone has watched you move is a guess. It may be a good guess, but it cannot account for the restriction in your left hip, the shoulder that only hurts under load, or the fact that your squat is being driven by the wrong muscles. Assessment is what turns a plan into your plan, and it is what makes progress measurable later rather than merely felt.

The Nine-Point Movement Screen

  • Overhead Squat Assessment — compensations in ankles, knees, hips, and shoulders under a loaded pattern
  • Single-Leg Squat — unilateral stability, hip strength, knee tracking
  • Push & Pull Patterns — shoulder mechanics and scapular stability under load
  • Posture Photos — front, side, and back baseline, re-scored quarterly
  • Joint Range of Motion — shoulders, hips, ankles, thoracic spine
  • Grip Strength — a reliable marker of functional strength, reviewed every 4 weeks
  • Bodystat Body Composition — medical-grade bioimpedance via the Bodystat QuadScan 4000: lean mass, fat mass, hydration
  • Core Stability — plank and anti-rotation testing
  • Gait Analysis — walking mechanics that reveal asymmetries and compensations

Resting Heart Rate and Blood Pressure

Two readings are taken at the diagnostic and repeated at each progress review: resting heart rate and resting blood pressure. Both are simple, both are free to collect, and both give context that a scale cannot.

  • Resting heart rate — measured seated after five minutes of quiet, tracked as a trend rather than a single number
  • Resting blood pressure — recorded the same way each time, same arm, same posture
  • Trend, not diagnosis — these are training-context markers, not a medical assessment

Readings outside the ordinary range are a referral, not a programming adjustment. If a reading is high or unusual, the next step is your physician, and training continues only within whatever guidance they give.

Education, not diagnosis: the screen describes how you move and gives us a baseline to program from. OmniFit Performance does not diagnose conditions. If something in the screen suggests a problem beyond a trainer's scope, we say so and refer.
Protocol

The Desk-Posture Protocol

Most in-home clients sit for a living, and the pattern that produces is consistent enough to program against directly. Prolonged sitting holds some muscle groups short while their opposing partners sit long and under-used, and tissue adapts to the position it spends the most time in. The protocol runs in every session rather than as a warm-up bolted on the front.

Release and Lengthen

Hip flexors, chest and front-of-shoulder, and the upper trapezius. Soft-tissue work followed by targeted lengthening, kept inside the 3/10 pain ceiling.

Activate

Glutes, deep core, lower trapezius, and the mid-back. These are the partners that go quiet during long sitting hours, and they will not switch on by themselves under load.

Integrate and Load

Hinge, squat, push, and pull retrained so the right muscles fire in the right order, then progressively loaded once the pattern holds. Load is added to a pattern that works, never to one that does not.

Scope

What We Can and Cannot Coach Around

These are the situations OmniFit Performance genuinely works with in-home, described honestly. None of this is treatment, and none of it replaces the provider managing your care.

Coached Regularly

  • Desk-related posture patterns — forward head, rounded shoulders, tight hip flexors
  • Post-physical-therapy return to training — after discharge and clearance
  • Old injuries that healed but never fully rebuilt — previous sprains, strains, and surgeries
  • Joint discomfort under load — programming modified around a 3/10 pain ceiling
  • Deconditioning after a long break — returning gradually with progression tied to tolerance

Only Alongside Your Provider

  • Ongoing physical therapy — we coordinate rather than duplicate, with your permission
  • Managed chronic conditions — training within whatever limits your physician sets
  • Recent surgery — only after written clearance, and at the pace your surgeon specifies

Not In Scope

  • Undiagnosed pain — see a physician or Doctor of Physical Therapy first
  • Acute injury — this is treatment, not training
  • Diagnosis of any kind — OmniFit does not diagnose, and does not prescribe rehabilitation
  • Prenatal and postpartum programming — not a certification we hold
The honest version: a trainer who claims every condition is workable is telling you something about their marketing, not their scope. If a situation belongs with a clinician, the useful thing we can do is say so early and coordinate once you are cleared.
Use Cases

What People Come to In-Home Coaching For

In-home coaching is rarely the goal on its own. These are the situations it usually attaches to. There is also a dedicated page on personal training after 50.

Long-Term Transformation

You want a body that holds up for years rather than a twelve-week push that unravels. The work is built to be repeatable: a plan you can still follow in month nine, progressions tied to what your body tolerates, and a coach who adjusts the plan rather than restarting it.

Fat Loss Without Crash Protocols

Aggressive plans usually fail on the joints or the schedule before they fail on willpower. Training your body tolerates is what makes consistency possible, and nutrition is coached alongside it with individualized macros rather than a template handed over on day one.

Desk-Worker Posture and Mobility

Long sitting hours, stiffness that arrives before the workday ends, and the sense of moving worse than you used to. Programming targets the specific patterns desk work creates, with corrective work built into every session rather than bolted on as a warm-up.

Data-Driven Hybrid Coaching

You travel, your calendar moves, and you want the work to continue anyway. In-person sessions combine with app-delivered programming, the Travel Protocol covers the weeks you're on the road, and the same benchmarks are tracked either way so nothing is guessed between visits.

In-Home Training FAQs

What areas do you serve for in-home training?

Do I need a home gym or my own equipment?
No. OmniFit Performance brings professional-grade portable equipment for a full session. All you need is about 8×8 feet of open floor space. If you already own a home gym, your program is built around your equipment instead, with recommendations for additions only when they'd genuinely help. During your consultation you can send photos of your space and we'll plan accordingly.
How is OmniFit different from a mobile trainer?
Mobile trainers typically bring a bag of bands and deliver a generic one-off workout. OmniFit Performance delivers a full, structured personal training program. You receive the same 9-point movement assessment, custom periodized programming, corrective exercise protocols, and accountability system used in the studio at Teqneeq Functional Health Center. The methodology is identical, and only the location changes.
What does a typical in-home session look like?
Sessions are 60 minutes. Each begins with a targeted warm-up and corrective activation sequence based on your assessment. The main training block focuses on compound strength movements, progressed systematically each week. Sessions end with mobility work addressing your specific desk posture issues. Everything is logged in the Trainerize app for tracking.
Can I combine in-home training with virtual coaching?
Yes. Many clients use a hybrid approach: in-home sessions for hands-on coaching and heavy lifts, plus app-guided programming for independent training days. This is especially effective for clients who travel. Your in-home sessions focus on corrective work and compound strength, while app sessions maintain consistency from hotel gyms or on the road. See Hybrid and Virtual Coaching.
What results can I expect?
Results vary by starting point and adherence, so OmniFit Performance measures every client against their own baseline rather than promising a timeline. Sustainable fat loss is tracked across 12 metrics, measured and self-reported, with progress reviews every 4 weeks (at least every 12 weeks on Train Only). Each review tracks posture photos, strength data, body composition, and adherence so you always know exactly where you stand.
Do I need prior training experience?
No. OmniFit Performance works with clients at every starting point, from complete beginners who have never touched a weight to experienced lifters who need corrective work after injuries. The 9-point movement assessment establishes your exact baseline, and your program is built from there. Every exercise is coached in person, progressed systematically, and tracked.
What if I travel for work?
The Travel Protocol covers it. You get travel-adapted programming in the app for hotel gyms and rooms, check-ins that continue at your Accountability Dial setting, and pause weeks when a trip can't hold training. During a travel week, a scheduled session can be taken live by video instead, included in your program. Your in-home sessions pick up again when you're back.
