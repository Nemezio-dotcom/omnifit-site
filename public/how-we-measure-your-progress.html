The Short Version

In Short

  • OmniFit Performance is a diagnostic-led coaching system that takes busy professionals over 30 from pain or plateau to lasting, independent strength.
  • Who it's for: anyone who wants progress shown in data, not described by feel.
  • What gets measured: body composition on a Bodystat QuadScan 4000 for in-studio clients, strength benchmarks, posture, mobility and well-being, always against your own baseline.
  • How much you track: it follows your Accountability Dial: daily logging at Full Accountability, a weekly written check-in at Coached, a one-tap weekly check-in on Train Only.
  • Reports: progress reviews every 4 weeks (at least every 12 weeks on Train Only), with aggregate client results published with their method on the Client Outcomes page.
  • Next step: a 45-minute consultation by video or phone. If it's a fit, the Performance Assessment comes next.
OmniFit tracks every client through the same seven-category framework, with measurement methods adapted to coaching format: body composition measured on a Bodystat QuadScan 4000 multi-frequency bioimpedance analyzer for in-studio clients, strength benchmarks, posture assessment scores, mobility function tests, session adherence rate, nutrition compliance, and subjective well-being — with progress reviews every 4 weeks (at least every 12 weeks on Train Only). This data-driven approach ensures no guesswork: each program adjustment is based on measurable trends rather than subjective impressions. Progress is judged against your own baseline rather than an average, so the same framework works whether you are starting from a long layoff or returning to structured training.
The Problem

The Measurement Gap

Without structured data, training gets judged by feel. Sessions are "tough," progress is described rather than measured, and the scale becomes the only benchmark. But scale weight alone misses the real picture — and without structured data, neither you nor your trainer can tell whether the program is actually working or needs adjustment.

At OmniFit Performance, we approach your program like a performance system. Every metric has a baseline and a defined reassessment cadence, with results reviewed at each progress review, every 4 weeks. If something stalls, the data can reveal the trend early so we can adjust before months are lost to an ineffective approach.

This is what separates structured coaching from a series of workouts. For clients pursuing substantial fat loss, we evaluate the trend over months rather than judging success from short-term scale changes.

The Three Pillars

Measurement Is the First Pillar of the Method

The OmniFit Method rests on three pillars that run through Assess, Correct, Build and Sustain. Measurement comes first because the other two depend on it: you cannot load a joint correctly or set the right accountability without a baseline.

Pillar 01

Measured Progress

The OmniFit Method measures before it programs. Every program begins with a Performance Assessment: nine movement benchmarks, posture photos, strength baselines, circumference measurements and a Bodystat QuadScan 4000 body-composition scan. Progress is judged against that baseline on a fixed cadence, never against an average, and the record is published with its method on the Client Outcomes page.

Pillar 02

Joint-First Strength

The OmniFit Method builds strength on a corrective floor. Corrective work sits in every warm-up, loading is chosen for what each joint tolerates under the 3/10 pain ceiling, and the patterns desk work creates are programmed for directly. For clients cleared from physical therapy, the Method is the bridge back to full training, within scope and never as treatment.

Pillar 03

Accountability That Travels

The OmniFit Method fits accountability to the client, not a template. The Accountability Dial has three settings, Full, Coached and Train Only, included at every tier (Executive Reset Bronze offers Coached or Train Only) and adjustable at any 4-week cycle boundary. Studio, in-home and virtual run on one app with pause weeks, and the Executive Reset carries the Method anywhere.

Tracking is a diagnostic tool, not a life sentence. We measure closely at the start so we can coach less over time. The goal is a client who no longer needs us every week. Full Accountability, with daily logging, is the default for the first cycles. The path from there is Full Accountability → Coached → Train Only → Tune-Up, which is graduation: one reassessment and training session per 4-week cycle, with programming through the app between visits. Sessions are for form, load and corrective work. The app carries your individual program between sessions, your logs and your check-ins — written for you, not a template.
The Framework

The 12 Metrics We Track for Every Client

Whether you train in-person at the studio inside Teqneeq FHC in 4S Ranch or through Hybrid and Virtual Coaching, every OmniFit client is tracked through the same seven-category framework. Body composition is measured on the QuadScan for studio clients and by guided self-measurement for virtual clients.

Category 01

Body Composition

Bioimpedance analysis on the Bodystat QuadScan 4000, plus tape measurements at 7 sites and circumference tracking. Scale weight alone cannot tell you whether a change is muscle, fat or fluid. The QuadScan helps separate them.

Category 02

Strength Benchmarks

Key compound lift performance (deadlift, squat, press, row), grip strength, and relative strength ratios. Reviewed every 4 weeks to ensure progressive overload is actually happening.

Category 03

Posture Assessment

Quarterly posture photos with structured scoring of common desk-posture patterns: rounded shoulders, forward head position, pelvic tilt and upper-back rounding. Built for desk-bound professionals.

Category 04

Mobility & Joint Function

Active range of motion tests for shoulders, hips, ankles, and thoracic spine. Tracked to show whether mobility and corrective work is changing how those joints move.

Category 05

Session Adherence

Percentage of scheduled sessions completed each 4-week cycle — including virtual and travel sessions. One of the strongest practical drivers of results.

Category 06

Nutrition Compliance

Weekly tracking of agreed-upon nutrition habits — protein intake, meal frequency, hydration, and behavior-change targets from behavior change coaching. Measured as percentage of weekly goals hit.

Category 07

Subjective Well-Being

Self-reported energy, sleep quality and stress levels — rated weekly. This captures what numbers can't and shows when recovery is falling behind training.

The Instrument

Measured Consistently, Not Guessed

Body composition is the metric most often guessed at and most often wrong. A bathroom scale reports one number and hides three. OmniFit measures it on a Bodystat QuadScan 4000, a multi-frequency bioelectrical impedance analyzer used in clinical and research settings.

Four frequencies

5, 50, 100 and 200 kHz. Lower-frequency measurements primarily reflect extracellular fluid, while higher-frequency measurements provide additional information used to estimate total and intracellular water. Multi-frequency impedance data supports separate estimates of intracellular and extracellular water.

Water, split properly

Intracellular and extracellular water reported separately rather than as one total. That helps show whether a weight change is more consistent with muscle, fat or fluid than scale weight alone can.

Phase angle

A 50 kHz measurement associated with cellular condition and hydration status. Tracked as a trend over time; it is not interpreted diagnostically.

Same device, same protocol

Every scan taken on the same device under standardized conditions, with calibration routinely verified, and read against your own previous readings rather than a population average.

The Bodystat QuadScan 4000 holds CE certification as a Class IIa medical device under MDD 93/42/EEC and has FDA clearance. OmniFit Performance uses it to measure body composition. It is not used to diagnose, treat or assess any medical condition, and OmniFit is not a medical provider.

Use Cases

What People Come to Coaching For

Coaching is rarely the goal on its own. These are the situations it usually attaches to.

Long-Term Transformation

You want a body that holds up for years rather than a twelve-week push that unravels. The work is built to be repeatable: a plan you can still follow in month nine, progressions tied to what your body tolerates, and a coach who adjusts the plan rather than restarting it.

Fat Loss Without Crash Protocols

Aggressive plans can be difficult to sustain around demanding schedules and individual training tolerance. Training your body tolerates is what makes consistency possible, and nutrition is coached alongside it with individualized macros rather than a template handed over on day one.

Desk-Worker Posture and Mobility

Long sitting hours, stiffness that arrives before the workday ends, and the sense of moving worse than you used to. Programming targets common mobility and strength patterns associated with prolonged sitting, with corrective work built into every session rather than bolted on as a warm-up.

Data-Driven Hybrid Coaching

You travel, your calendar moves, and you want the work to continue anyway. In-person sessions combine with app-delivered programming, the Travel Protocol covers the weeks you're on the road, and the same benchmarks are tracked either way so nothing is guessed between visits.
