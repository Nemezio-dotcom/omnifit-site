What the Scan Measures

Data a Bathroom Scale Will Never Give You

The scale reports one number and hides everything that matters. Two people can weigh exactly the same and have completely different bodies — and two "identical" weigh-ins can hide muscle loss behind a shift in water. Reading at four frequencies, the QuadScan 4000 measures the fluid compartments separately, so a change in weight can be attributed to fat, to lean tissue or to fluid instead of inferred from the total.

Fat Mass vs. Lean Mass

The core split, estimated from the scan: how much of your weight is body fat versus muscle, bone, and organ tissue. This is the number that tells you whether a diet is working — or just shrinking you.

Hydration & Fluid Balance

Total body water plus the ratio of fluid inside versus outside your cells (ICW/ECW). Helps explain day-to-day weight swings; read as a trend alongside how you feel and recover.

Phase Angle

A cellular-health marker measured directly by the device — higher values generally reflect stronger cell membrane integrity and better training readiness. Tracked at every rescan.

FFMI & Trend Data

Fat-Free Mass Index puts your muscularity in context of your height — a far better benchmark than BMI. Every rescan builds your trend line, which is where the real answers live.

On a GLP-1 Medication?

Is Your Weight Loss Fat — or Muscle?

Rapid weight loss on GLP-1 medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide can take a significant amount of lean mass with it — and the scale can't tell you it's happening. Body composition testing can. OmniFit tracks estimated fat mass and lean mass separately at every rescan, so you and your prescriber know whether the weight coming off is the weight you wanted to lose — and your training and protein targets adjust to protect the muscle you have.

Baseline Before or During

Whether you're about to start a GLP-1 or months in, the first scan gives you a clear, repeatable read on where your lean mass stands today — the estimate everything else is compared against.

Lean Mass Protection

Rescans every 4–8 weeks catch muscle loss early. If lean mass is dropping, resistance training frequency and protein intake adjust immediately — before the loss compounds.

Common Questions

Body Composition Testing FAQ

How much does body composition testing cost in San Diego at OmniFit?
The Performance Assessment is $150 and includes a Bodystat QuadScan 4000 scan reading at four frequencies — fat mass, lean mass, body cell mass, intracellular and extracellular water, phase angle and segmental analysis — a 9-point movement screen, and expert interpretation of your results. The $150 is credited toward your first program, Reset cycle or 5-pack. There are no activation fees.
What is the Bodystat QuadScan 4000?
The Bodystat QuadScan 4000 is a multi-frequency bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) device that measures body composition by passing a safe, imperceptible electrical signal through the body. Reading at four frequencies — 5, 50, 100 and 200 kHz — it reads impedance and phase angle directly and separates intracellular from extracellular water; fat mass, lean mass, body cell mass and total body water are estimates calculated from those readings, which multi-frequency analysis makes more robust than a single-frequency reading. It's the same class of device used in clinical and research settings, not a consumer gym kiosk.
How is this different from a DEXA scan?
DEXA uses low-dose X-ray imaging and is the research gold standard for a single-snapshot measurement, typically costing $100–200 per scan in San Diego. The Bodystat uses bioimpedance — no radiation — and reads at four frequencies, which is how it measures the intracellular and extracellular water compartments and phase angle that DEXA does not report at all. The bigger difference is context: OmniFit scans are interpreted by the coach who writes your program and repeated every 4–8 weeks on the same calibrated device, so your trend line drives real programming decisions rather than sitting in a PDF.
I'm on a GLP-1 medication. Why does body composition testing matter?
Rapid weight loss on GLP-1 medications can include significant lean mass loss, and a scale can't distinguish fat from muscle. Regular body composition testing gives you a clear, repeatable read on what kind of weight you're losing, so resistance training and protein intake can be adjusted to protect muscle — and gives you trend data to share with your prescriber. This is one of the most common reasons clients book the assessment.
How should I prepare for the scan?
For the most accurate reading: avoid eating for 2–3 hours beforehand, skip alcohol for 24 hours, avoid intense exercise immediately before, and arrive normally hydrated. Consistency matters more than perfection — we schedule your rescans under similar conditions so your trend data stays clean.
How often should I rescan?
Every 4–8 weeks, depending on your goal. Body composition changes too slowly for weekly scans to mean anything, and quarterly gaps miss course-correction windows. Active OmniFit clients rescan as a built-in checkpoint — it's the accountability layer of the program, not an add-on.

In-home assessments are also available throughout North County and coastal San Diego — including Rancho Santa Fe, Carmel Valley, Del Mar, Encinitas, Carlsbad, and La Jolla. No travel fee in Carmel Valley, Santaluz, Fairbanks Ranch, Rancho Bernardo, 4S Ranch or Poway; elsewhere in our in-home service area a travel fee applies, confirmed before you book.
